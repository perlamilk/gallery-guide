Mead Art Museum “My beautiful, pitiful age”: The 20th century through the Thomas P. Whitney Collection

On view August 25, 2026–January 3, 2027

Image

Isaak Levitan, The Steppe, late 19th century. Pastel on paper mounted on cardboard. AC 2001.599. Gift of Thomas P. Whitney (Class of 1937)

The multipart exhibition at the Mead Art Museum and the Amherst Center for Russian Culture will offer the first in-depth exploration of the Thomas P. Whitney Collection of art and rare books. Exhibition materials will be shown in galleries in both locations throughout the 2026-2027 academic year:

  • “My beautiful, pitiful age”: The 20th century through the Thomas P. Whitney Collection (August 25, 2026—January 3, 2027)
  • Anchors and Aspirations: The 20th century through the Thomas P. Whitney Collection (February 23—July 3, 2027)
Image

Naum Gabo, Vertical Construction No. 2 (The Waterfall), 1965-1966. Bronze with stainless steel spring wire. AC 2001.600. Gift of Thomas P. Whitney (Class of 1937)

A graduate of Amherst College, Thomas P. Whitney (1917–2007, Class of 1937) worked as an economic analyst and then as a diplomat and journalist in Moscow from 1944 to 1953. Upon returning to the United States, he became known as an expert in Soviet affairs and a translator of Russian literature. Driven by a special interest in Russian-speaking networks of thought and visual art, he assembled a library of rare books, manuscripts, and periodicals that formed an intellectual whole with his art holdings. The literary materials established the Amherst Center for Russian Culture in 1991, followed a decade later by the gift to the Mead of approximately 700 artworks by over 160 artists from the Russian Empire and Soviet Union.

Today, these holdings remain one of the most comprehensive North American collections of art and literary materials from that region. The art collection encompasses the late Russian Empire and the Soviet Union while tracing artists' global trajectories across the territories of present-day Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, the Baltic states, Germany, France, the United Kingdom, the United States, and beyond. Holdings include Orthodox icons from various periods and regions, as well as the artwork of important late 19th- and early 20th-century figures like Léon Bakst, Alexandre Benois, and Nicholas Roerich; radical modernists including Vassily Kandinsky, Kazimir Malevich, Naum Gabo, Alexander Archipenko, Sonia Delaunay, Natalia Goncharova, Alexandra Exter; as well as major Soviet nonconformists like Vladimir Veisberg, Oskar Rabin, and Ülo Sooster. The collection combines paintings, drawings, prints, sculptures, rare books, and manuscripts, with a special focus on the dialogue between visual art and literature.

Echoing this connection, the exhibition title refers to the poem “The Age" (1922) by the Russian poet Osip Mandelstam. He portrays the twentieth century as a weak, cruel beast for which he feels compassion: initially viewed as an age of beauty and progress, it incurred harm and gave rise to some of the most catastrophic events in human history. The Whitney collection itself emerged from the Cold War, which was one of the century's defining political conflicts. We now revisit this collection in another turbulent era, one in which international politics and cultural relations are complicated by the ongoing Russian invasion of Ukraine and the rise of autocracies around the world. The exhibition engages with the questions of how cultural artifacts connected to this region can be studied, taught, and displayed at this fraught moment.

Since Whitney envisioned his gift as a transformative contribution to the College—with the potential to grow and change in scope—a portion of the newly-acquired materials will be included in the exhibition to indicate the directions in which the collection has expanded.

About the Exhibition

"My beautiful, pitiful age": The 20th century through the Thomas P. Whitney Collection was curated by Maria Timina (Curator of Russian and European Art, Mead Art Museum). This exhibition is made possible with the support of the Amherst Whitney Collection of Russian Art Fund, the Julia A. Whitney Fund for Russian Art, the John F. Wieland '58 Director's Discretionary Fund, the Hall and Kate Peterson Fund, and the David W. Mesker '53 Fund, as well as the generosity of the Office of the Provost and Dean of the Faculty and the Friends of the Mead.

A second part to the show, Anchors and Aspirations, will debut in Spring 2027 (Feb. 23-July 3, 2027).

Programming

All are invited to join for the exhibition programs, which are free and open to the public. 

Exhibition Opening Reception @ the Mead Art Museum
Thursday, September 10 (5-7 PM) 

"How Russian is 'Russian Art'?" Scholar Panel @ the ACRC (202 Webster Hall)  
Friday, September 11 (11 AM-12:30 PM) 

Curator Maria Timina will be joined by two eminent art historians, Jane Sharp (Rutgers University) and Maria Taroutina (Brown University), to discuss how Russia’s imperial history bears on how we view Whitney’s collection today.

Curator Tours

Thursday, September 24 (6-7 PM) at the  Mead
Wednesday, October 14 (1-2 PM) @ the ACRC (202 Webster Hall)
Thursday, November 19 (5-6 PM) at the Mead

Curator Maria Timina will lead guided walkthroughs of the exhibition galleries at both the Mead Art Museum and the corresponding gallery of the Amherst Center for Russian Culture, concluding with a Q&A. The ACRC galleries focus on Whitney's books and literary materials, while the Mead is presenting majority artworks of various mediums.

"Memories of Place and Migration" with Es Malagon-Martinez

friday, october 9 (1-2 PM) 
Wednesday, October 21 (1-2 PM) 

Join Advanced Student Museum Educator Es Malagon-Martinez for a talk examining the thematic links between artworks produced in varied contexts. Drawing on assemblage, sculpture, painting, 3-D printing, and lithographic works, the gallery walk will bridge both exhibitions currently on view through the lenses of place, movement, and time.
 

"Art and the State" with exiled Russian artist Victoria Lomasko
Thursday, October 29 (5-6 PM) 

Victoria Lomasko leads a guided tour of “My beautiful, pitiful age” in the context of the Thomas P. Whitney Collection. Many of the collection’s 20th-century artists worked outside their homeland, and some openly dissented against the regime. Lomasko will reflect on how their situation rhymes with her own as an exiled dissident artist today.

At Whitney's Table: Russia in Litchfield County," a talk by Honor Moore
Thursday, November 5 (4:30-6 PM) 

The writer Honor Moore will give a talk about her memories of her former neighbor, Thomas Whitney, and the vibrant circle surrounding him, including artists and writers such as Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, Andrei Voznesensky, Inge Morath, Henry Miller, and more.  

 

 

Audio guide for “My beautiful, pitiful age”

Listen to a guided tour of the exhibition narrated by two of the Mead’s interns, Amelia Kwak ’29 and Audrey Yoo ’28.
